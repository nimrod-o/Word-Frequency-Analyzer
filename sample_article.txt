Kenya's Youth Turn to Digital Skills as Demand for Tech Talent Grows

NAKURU — Across Kenya, a growing number of young people are enrolling in short technology courses, hoping to turn digital skills into stable income. Training centres in Nakuru, Kisumu and Nairobi report that enrollment in courses on office applications, graphic design and data analysis has risen sharply over the past two years. About 3,000 learners have joined such programmes in the region alone.

Instructors say the trend reflects a change in how employers hire. Many companies now value practical skills over long academic credentials, and a well-built portfolio can matter as much as a certificate. "Employers want to see what a candidate can actually build," said one instructor at a Nakuru training centre. "It's not enough to say you know a tool. You have to show the work."

The rise of artificial intelligence has added new urgency. Training centres are introducing AI fluency courses that teach students how to use tools responsibly, check their output, and apply them to everyday tasks in business and education. Students who once worried that automation would replace them now see it as a skill to master.

Challenges remain. Internet costs are high, reliable electricity is not guaranteed in every town, and many learners cannot afford a laptop of their own. Some centres have responded by offering shared computer labs and flexible payment plans, allowing students to pay for training in small instalments.

Education officials say the government is watching the sector closely. Regulators want training providers to align their courses with national standards, including the diploma programmes offered through the Kenya National Examinations Council, so that graduates hold qualifications that employers recognise.
